[2026-04-20T02:17:16.9480000-05:00] the_biggest_rick: The Three Timeframes in a Triple Momentum System

Short-term (1 month or less) — Reversal

This is the least reliable for longs. The 1-month reversal effect (Jegadeesh & Titman, 1993 and others) shows that recent short-term winners tend to underperform in the near term. This is largely attributed to microstructure noise, liquidity effects, and short-term overreaction. If anything, this signal is more useful for short positions — fading the recent spike.

Medium-term (3–12 months, classically ~6–12 month lookback, skip 1 month) — Strongest Sustained Trend

This is the core of documented momentum. The classic Jegadeesh & Titman finding shows winners keep winning and losers keep losing over this horizon. This is your strongest case for long-only positions, and also the most robust signal for long/short paired trades. Most serious momentum systems (including Antonacci’s Dual Momentum) anchor to this window. The “skip 1 month” convention is deliberate — it strips out the short-term reversal contamination.

Long-term (36–60 months) — Reversal
De Bondt & Thaler’s research shows that over multi-year horizons, past winners underperform and past losers outperform. This is mean reversion at a slow scale, likely driven by fundamental valuation reassertion. 

Long-term signals are therefore better suited for short signals on former winners or long signals on former losers — essentially contrarian positioning, not trend following.

Motivation

Existing momentum literature is limited in scope. Most studies focus on a single lookback window, a single asset class, or a single directional bias. 

There is little to no documented research applying differentiated positional roles across multiple momentum horizons simultaneously. This represents a gap worth exploring.
Core Concept

A triple-horizon momentum ranking system where each timeframe is assigned a distinct role and set of permitted positions, rather than treating all horizons as additive signals.

Horizon Structure
Short-term momentum — Long and short positions permitted. The signal captures both trend continuation and short-term reversal candidates, with supplementary indicators such as RSI or rate-of-change used to differentiate between the two.

Medium-term momentum — Long positions only. This is the most well-supported horizon in existing literature and serves as the anchor of the system.

Long-term momentum — Long and short positions permitted, driven by overbought and oversold conditions. A ranking system combined with exhaustion indicators (such as RSI or percentile-based measures) is used to identify mean reversion candidates.
Supporting Factors

The system may be enhanced by overlaying:
    •    Macroeconomic regime data
    •    Value factors
    •    Other cross-sectional or time-series filters
Goal

To build a generalized, rules-based system that performs consistently across multiple asset classes — equities, commodities, fixed income, and potentially others — without being overfit to any single market.
