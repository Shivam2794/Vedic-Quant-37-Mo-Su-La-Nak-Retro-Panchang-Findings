[2025-12-25T11:03:03.3950000-06:00] the_biggest_rick: There is a fundamental relationship between optimization and robustness. Increasing robustness almost always requires increasing aggregation. When performance is evaluated across parameter neighborhoods, the optimization itself becomes aggregated, shifting the process away from isolated optima and toward stable regions of parameter space. This reduces sensitivity to noise and discourages reliance on sharp, fragile peaks.

The same trade-off appears at the indicator level. Adaptive indicators such as KAMA or ER-based constructs adjust to volatility regimes and therefore tend to produce higher peak Sharpe values, but they are also more prone to overfitting. Smoother indicators such as EMA, SMA, or MACD are structurally slower and less expressive, which causes them to overfit far more slowly, even if this typically results in lower standalone out-of-sample Sharpe. In this sense, higher Sharpe values should be treated as a warning signal rather than an objective, as they are more likely to reflect curve fitting than structural edge.

Similar considerations apply when combining logic sets. Jointly grid-searching multiple logic components into a single optimized system can improve Sharpe, but can be at the cost of robustness (though, SMOOTHERS will still survive, they're slow and not very dynamic unlike a KAMA). Separating logic sets and allowing them to interact through veto mechanisms introduces conservatism, yet it reduces the probability that a single overfit component dominates decision-making. Even imperfect or partially overfit components can improve robustness when they are able to veto each other rather than act independently.

[2025-12-25T11:03:06.4090000-06:00] the_biggest_rick: A further limitation exists outside the model and the optimization process: strategy performance is strongly dependent on the underlying asset. Strategies optimized on assets with strong structural drift, such as NVIDIA or Tesla, will almost inevitably appear superior to those applied to more stable or mean-reverting assets, even when the underlying signal quality is comparable. An equity with persistent upward momentum cannot be meaningfully compared to Apple over the same horizon, and neither can be compared to a defensive asset such as Coca-Cola. These assets exhibit fundamentally different return distributions, volatility structures, and regime behaviors. As a result, Sharpe ratios are implicitly shaped by the asset’s return profile rather than solely by the quality of the strategy. Direct comparisons across such assets are therefore misleading unless the asset-level characteristics are explicitly normalized or controlled for.

These external influences cannot be resolved through optimization alone and must be addressed at the portfolio or allocation level. Optimization performed at the single-asset level will tend to absorb asset drift into the strategy parameters, creating the illusion of signal strength where none may exist independently of the underlying trend.

For these reasons, early-stage development should not focus on optimization. When working with a single signal or logic set, the priority should robustness rather than peak performance. If sensitivity analysis and neighborhood aggregation produce a strategy with a Sharpe around one, that is sufficient. At that point, the strategy is a viable building block. Robustness then emerges through additional layers, including multiple logic sets, ensembles across assets, long and short exposures, and ranking or allocation mechanisms. Together, these layers reduce degrees of freedom and limit overfitting, allowing the resulting portfolio to remain viable over extended periods despite non-stationarity.

[2026-07-04T03:25:14.3630000-05:00] xyrus1418: def generate_bbands_signals(self, close: pd.Series, **kwargs) -> Tuple[pd.Series, pd.Series]:
        window = self.params.get("window", 20)
        num_std = self.params.get("num_std", 2.0)

        sma = close.rolling(window).mean()
        std = close.rolling(window).std(ddof=0)
        upper = sma + num_std * std
        middle = sma

        entries = close.vbt.crossed_above(upper)
        entries = entries.reindex(close.index).fillna(False).astype(bool)

        exits = close.vbt.crossed_below(middle)
        exits = exits.reindex(close.index).fillna(False).astype(bool)

        return entries, exits
----------------------------------------
[2026-07-04T03:25:40.8140000-05:00] xyrus1418: def generate_aroon_signals(self, close: pd.Series, **kwargs) -> Tuple[pd.Series, pd.Series]:
        period = self.params.get("period", 25)
        up_threshold = self.params.get("up_threshold", 70)
        down_threshold = self.params.get("down_threshold", 70)

        high = kwargs.get("high", close)
        low = kwargs.get("low", close)

        aroon_down, aroon_up = talib.AROON(
            high.values.astype(np.float64),
            low.values.astype(np.float64),
            timeperiod=period,
        )

        aroon_up_series = pd.Series(aroon_up, index=close.index)
        aroon_down_series = pd.Series(aroon_down, index=close.index)

        _up_above = aroon_up_series > up_threshold
        entries = (_up_above & ~_up_above.shift(1, fill_value=False)).astype(bool)
        _down_above = aroon_down_series > down_threshold
        exits = (_down_above & ~_down_above.shift(1, fill_value=False)).astype(bool)

        return entries, exits
----------------------------------------
