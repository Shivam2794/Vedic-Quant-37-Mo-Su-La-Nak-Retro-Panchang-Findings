The Theoretical Limits and Master Architecture for High-Frequency Option Price Reconstruction
Introduction: The Epistemological and Mathematical Bounds of Synthetic Generation
The endeavor to reconstruct historical minute-by-minute option data from aggregate end-of-day implied volatility (IV) surfaces, discrete historical prices, and ancillary market information constitutes one of the most mathematically profound inverse problems in modern quantitative finance. The theoretical ambition to achieve an exact 99.99% accuracy rate relative to the unobserved, true historical minute-by-minute execution data requires an exhaustive examination of the boundaries between continuous-time stochastic calculus, deep generative machine learning, and the discrete friction of market microstructure.
To address the feasibility condition immediately: achieving a literal 99.99% point-to-point Euclidean accuracy against actual historical microstructure data is fundamentally impossible due to the stochastic nature of high-frequency market noise.1 In deterministic engineering and specific machine learning domains—such as Internet of Things (IoT) anomaly detection, biological sensor data classification, and medical imaging—deep learning architectures frequently achieve and exceed 99.99% accuracy.3 However, financial markets operate under a different epistemological framework. In financial econometrics, the observed transaction price is not the true "efficient" or fundamental price of the asset. Rather, the observed price is a composite of the unobservable efficient martingale price and a market microstructure noise component, driven by discrete tick sizes, bid-ask bounce, order flow imbalances, and sub-millisecond latency arbitrage.8 As the sampling frequency increases to the minute-by-minute or tick-by-tick level, the variance of this microstructure noise completely overwhelms the variance of the true price signal.1 Consequently, aiming for 99.99% accuracy against raw transaction data implies an attempt to deterministically reverse-engineer pure stochastic noise.
Despite this fundamental boundary, generating a synthetic high-frequency dataset that is 99.99% statistically indistinguishable from real market data is computationally and theoretically viable.12 This means the synthetic data will preserve the exact joint probability distributions, volatility smiles, forward-looking skewness, cross-correlations, and strict no-arbitrage bounds of the true historical market.15 By integrating continuous-time stochastic volatility models, advanced Fourier transform inverse regularization, arbitrage-free volatility surface parameterizations (such as Surface SVI), and modern generative artificial intelligence (Variational Autoencoders and Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks), it is possible to construct a highly detailed architectural master plan. This plan facilitates the synthesis of high-frequency option paths that adhere to the strictest mathematical constraints of modern quantitative finance, effectively bridging the gap between theoretical asset pricing and empirical microstructure.
The Inverse Problem in Option Pricing
The core challenge of generating historic option prices from an implied volatility surface is fundamentally an ill-posed inverse problem. Forward option pricing utilizes a known volatility dynamic to compute a specific derivative price; inverse pricing utilizes observed cross-sectional option prices to extract the underlying volatility dynamics, or more specifically, the market's implied risk-neutral probability density function.16

Arbitrage-Free Implied Volatility Surface Interpolation
To generate minute-by-minute pricing, the sparse, discrete grid of daily historical option prices (which only exist at specific listed strikes and maturities) must be mapped into a continuous, dense, and rigorously arbitrage-free implied volatility surface (IVS). The generated synthetic surface must absolutely preclude calendar spread arbitrage (meaning options with longer maturities must command higher time value) and butterfly arbitrage (ensuring the risk-neutral density remains non-negative, which corresponds to the strict convexity of call prices with respect to strike).23
The Instability of Spline Interpolation
Historically, practitioners and exchanges utilized cubic spline interpolation in delta space to connect volatility points and extrapolate the wings.25 While computationally inexpensive and capable of perfectly hitting every observed market quote, splines are highly prone to oscillatory behavior in deep out-of-the-money regions.15 These mathematical "wiggles" inevitably result in a violation of the convex shape required for no-arbitrage bounds, yielding negative implied probabilities and rendering the surface useless for the rigorous generation of synthetic historical data.15
The SABR Model vs. SVI Parameterization
To address the limitations of non-parametric splines, parametric models inspired by stochastic dynamics were introduced. The Stochastic Alpha, Beta, Rho (SABR) model is a dynamic model where both the forward price and its volatility evolve over time.28 While SABR accurately models the implied volatility smile at specific maturities and provides relatively stable parameter estimates over time, it struggles when the implied volatility curve exhibits higher-order asymmetry or when the risk-neutral probability density function exhibits a bimodal form (such as preceding an earnings announcement).30

Subsequent advancements led to the Surface SVI (SSVI) model, which established mathematically provable parameter constraints that guarantee the absolute elimination of both butterfly and calendar spread arbitrage across the entire continuous surface.33 In the proposed master architecture for synthetic generation, SSVI acts as the foundational, non-negotiable constraint layer. No generated minute-by-minute option price can imply a volatility that violates the SSVI bounds established by the historical daily anchor points.

The Microstructure Mechanics of High-Frequency Pricing
Transitioning from a mathematically continuous volatility surface to a discrete, minute-by-minute pricing grid requires modeling the profound frictions of reality. In high-frequency environments, the theoretical efficient price is unobservable; the quoted option price is heavily impacted by bid-ask spreads, tick-size constraints, and order flow imbalance.9
Disentangling Market Microstructure Noise
High-frequency market microstructure noise is primarily explained by linear models where the trade direction (whether a trade is buyer or seller initiated) is multiplied by the dynamic quoted bid-ask spread.11 The tick-to-spread ratio determines the degree of price discreteness, which serves as a massive residual source of noise.11 In options markets, high-frequency trading activity in the underlying equity market directly transmits into the derivatives market by increasing the hedging costs of the options market-maker.37 This leads to wider bid-ask spreads and a higher price impact for large options transactions.37
To accurately synthesize these phenomena in reverse engineering, continuous-time diffusion models must be extended into discrete-time binomial or multinomial tree models that incorporate Grossman-Stiglitz market frictions and history dependence.13 Modern research demonstrates that binomial trees augmented with path-dependent transition probabilities—estimated via Random Forest classifiers trained on high-frequency limit order book data—can successfully capture microstructure effects while preserving no-arbitrage conditions.13 Furthermore, models analyzing the dynamic price impact of trade sizes document a nonlinear positive relationship between absolute returns and cumulative trading volume.41
Tick Size Constraints and Informational Efficiency
The minimum tick size mandated by exchanges creates a rigid pricing grid that artificially delays price discovery.8 For instance, if the theoretical, arbitrage-free Black-Scholes price of an option at exactly 10:14:00 AM is $1.034, but the exchange mandates a $0.05 minimum price variation (MPV), the market will quote either $1.00 or $1.05.10 Options traders attempt to use price-improving quotes to incorporate new information, but tick size constraints inhibit this process, resulting in lagged updates to the midpoint price.43
Therefore, a 99.99% theoretically accurate model price will inevitably display a tracking error against the empirical historical price due strictly to this structural quantization error.2 To construct synthetic data that accurately reflects historical realities, artificial quantization layers—incorporating specific regulatory regimes like the Penny Pilot Program—must be applied to the theoretically pure reverse-engineered prices.2
Deep Generative Architectures for Synthetic Synthesis
Because traditional econometric models (such as ARIMA or generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) frameworks) cannot simultaneously capture the multidimensional complexities of the volatility smile, the term structure, and the non-linear intraday price jumps, advanced machine learning paradigms must be deployed.45 While GARCH models provide excellent flexibility in representing higher moments like variance 48, they lack the dimensionality required for full surface generation.
Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and GANs
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have revolutionized implied volatility surface reconstruction and synthetic financial data generation.12 A VAE can be utilized to compress the massive dimensionality of historical volatility surfaces (spanning hundreds of strike-maturity pairs) into a dense, highly compact low-dimensional latent representation (e.g., a 10-dimensional latent space).49
The GAN architecture is extraordinarily effective for synthesis because it leverages an adversarial training paradigm. A Generator network creates synthetic implied volatility surfaces or time-series paths, while a Discriminator network attempts to distinguish these synthetic surfaces from true historical market data.12 To ensure financial viability and theoretical soundness, these GANs are augmented with hard domain-specific constraints and soft penalty functions during training, penalizing the loss function if the generated surfaces exhibit calendar or butterfly arbitrage.12 Architectures like the Volatility Neural Process (VolNP) can reconstruct a complete, arbitrage-free IVS from a highly sparse set of intraday context quotes in a single forward pass, bypassing the need for computationally heavy numerical root-finding optimizations.51
TimeGANs, Deep Signatures, and Rough Volatility
To generate the sequential, minute-by-minute temporal evolution of these latent surfaces, Time-series GANs (TimeGAN) and Autoregressive State-Space models are deployed.52 TimeGAN explicitly models both nonlinear and high-dimensional temporal structures, capturing the complex temporal dependencies and transition probabilities of the financial time series through recurrent mechanisms.52
Furthermore, recent breakthroughs in quantitative finance demonstrate that volatility is rough. The time series of historical log-volatilities is remarkably well approximated by a fractional Brownian motion with a Hurst parameter of .54 To capture this extreme roughness, Deep Signature Kernels are employed.55 By using the signature of a path (an iterated sequence of integrals), the machine learning model maps the non-Markovian, rough volatility paths into a linear tensor algebra space, allowing for the highly precise generation of minute-by-minute stochastic local volatility dynamics.56 Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), optimized via Bayesian Optimization for Penalized Spike Accuracy, can further enhance the forecasting and generation of high-frequency price spikes inherent in these rough regimes.58
Addressing Class Imbalance and Rare Events
In generating multi-year synthetic datasets, the system must accurately model tail events (e.g., flash crashes, extreme gap downs). Techniques utilizing Wasserstein GANs with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP) or Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Techniques (SMOTE) integrated with k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) imputation are critical for addressing the severe class imbalance of rare market events, ensuring that the synthetic dataset captures the "fat tails" characteristic of empirical asset distributions without introducing artificial noise.47
The Grand Master Architecture: A Detailed Computational Plan
To definitively execute the mandate of creating minute-by-minute historical option data from aggregate parameters with maximal theoretical accuracy, the following six-phase grand master architecture details the exact mathematical and computational pipeline required.
Phase 1: Macro-Anchor Calibration and IVS Surface Completion
Objective: Establish a mathematically pure, continuous, and completely arbitrage-free boundary condition for the open and close of each historical trading day.
Data Ingestion & Cleansing: Ingest all available historical EOD option prices, underlying asset prices, risk-free interest rate curves, and implied dividend yield term structures.62 Filter out options that breach American-style bounds or have bid prices of zero.64
SSVI Calibration: Fit the discrete, filtered EOD data to the Surface Stochastic Volatility Inspired (SSVI) parameterization.32 Utilize quasi-Newton updates or Levenberg-Marquardt optimization to extract the core parameters  for each maturity slice.32
Arbitrage Penalty Execution: Apply Gatheral’s theoretical bounds to the calibrated parameters. Ensure the risk-neutral density function remains strictly positive () across the entire continuum.23 This creates an impenetrable "Anchor Surface" for the day.
Phase 2: Latent State Encoding via Variational Autoencoders
Objective: Translate the rigid SSVI continuous surface into a dynamic, low-dimensional neural framework capable of fluid intraday morphing.
Surface Discretization: Sample the calibrated SSVI Anchor Surface into a standardized, dense  grid (e.g., 40 maturities  100 deltas).49
VAE Compression: Pass the dense grid through a deep Variational Autoencoder. The encoder compresses the vast dimensionality of the surface into a 10-dimensional multivariate Gaussian latent distribution .49
Latent Mapping: The entire day's volatility state is now mathematically represented by a concise latent vector, drastically reducing the computational burden for the subsequent minute-by-minute temporal generation steps.49
Phase 3: High-Frequency Temporal Path Generation
Objective: Generate the minute-by-minute evolution of both the underlying asset price and the latent volatility vector between the established daily anchors.
Underlying Asset Path Generation: Using the known historical daily open, high, low, and close (OHLC) of the underlying asset, generate minute-by-minute stochastic paths. To accurately capture the fat tails, volume correlations, and volatility clustering of high-frequency data, deploy a conditional TimeGAN model.46 The TimeGAN is strictly conditioned on the macroscopic OHLC bounds to ensure the generated minute paths seamlessly connect to the known historical aggregates without destroying temporal dependencies.46
Rough Volatility Evolution: Evolve the latent volatility vector  minute-by-minute. Because intraday volatility is non-Markovian, utilize a Deep Signature Transform model operating under a rough volatility paradigm (Hurst parameter ).54 This generates an intraday trajectory of the implied volatility surface that realistically reacts to the generated underlying asset movements.
Forward Decoding: Pass each minute's generated latent vector  back through the VAE Decoder to produce a complete, continuous implied volatility surface for every minute of the trading day.49
Phase 4: Continuous Pricing and Reverse Engineering
Objective: Compute the theoretical option prices from the generated minute-by-minute volatility surfaces.
Continuous Pricing: For every minute, and for every available strike and maturity on the option chain, feed the generated underlying price  and the decoded implied volatility  into the generalized Black-Scholes PDE (or the Carr-Madan FFT formula for jump-diffusion considerations) to compute the continuous theoretical mid-price .16
Dividend and Rate Integration: Continuously adjust the forward price  using the interpolated minute-by-minute risk-free rate and the implied dividend yield term structure, ensuring exact cost-of-carry parity.62
Phase 5: Microstructure Injection and Quantization
Objective: Translate the pure, continuous theoretical prices into realistic, discrete market quotes by simulating market friction.
Bid-Ask Spread Simulation: Apply an order flow and liquidity model. Calculate the dynamic bid-ask spread  as a function of the underlying stock's spread, the option's specific liquidity, time to maturity, and the option's Greek exposures (Vega and Gamma).10
Tick-Size Quantization: Introduce the final microstructure noise matrix. Apply the specific historical exchange regulatory tick size rounding logic (e.g., $0.01 for options under $3.00, $0.05 for options over $3.00) to the theoretical bounds to yield the final synthetic  and  prices.10 This step explicitly mimics the informational delay caused by structural constraints.2
Phase 6: Validation and Anomaly Filtering
Objective: Ensure the generated high-frequency dataset is statistically robust and free of systemic errors.
Arbitrage Verification: Pass the final minute-by-minute dataset through a rigorous constraints checker to ensure no generated minute violates fundamental financial mechanics (e.g., European option lower bounds, call/put parity).69
Distributional Tracking Error Analysis: Evaluate the synthetic data against known historical aggregates using metrics such as the root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE).12 The generated data must exhibit a tracking error that aligns perfectly with theoretical expectations of microstructure noise.1
Strategic Implications and Conclusion
The development of a minute-by-minute synthetic option dataset from historical parameters represents a monumental convergence of quantitative finance theory and generative artificial intelligence. While achieving an exact 99.99% deterministic match to historical prints is a statistical impossibility due to the overriding presence of market microstructure noise and quantization constraints, synthesizing data that is 99.99% statistically accurate and mathematically pure is achievable.
By executing the detailed master architecture—anchoring the data via arbitrage-free SSVI boundaries, compressing the surface topology via Variational Autoencoders, evolving the temporal dynamics through rough volatility Deep Signature models and TimeGANs, and systematically injecting realistic microstructure frictions via Random Forest transitions—the resulting dataset transcends simple curve fitting. It provides a massive, scalable, and rigorously robust foundation. This computationally generated data allows for the exhaustive backtesting of high-frequency trading strategies, the stress-testing of complex derivatives portfolios, and the training of advanced deep learning algorithms in environments where historical data is sparse, sensitive, or incomplete. The paradigm shifts from attempting to guess the exact noise of the past, to perfectly reconstructing the underlying financial mechanics that generated it.
